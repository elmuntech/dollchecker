content at all, so almost every answer is "no" — the ones that need thought are
the last few, and they are the ones that decide whether you end up in a
children's category you do not want to be in.

### Target audience and content (the section that matters)

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Target age groups | **18 and over only** |
| Could the app appeal to children? | **No.** It is a decision-support tool for adults buying and managing toys. There is no game, no character, no reward for a child. |
| Do you want to opt into Designed for Families? | **No** |
| Does the app contain a "Made for children" ad experience? | Not applicable — no ads |

> Declaring an adults-only target audience is deliberate and honest. The app
> holds a child's first name and optional date of birth, entered by the parent,
> and the listing and privacy policy say so plainly — but the *user* is an
> adult, and opting into the Families programme would bring obligations
> (COPPA/GDPR-K, ad-content rules, teacher review) that do not match the
> product.

### Apple's AI disclosure

App Review asks how generative AI is used and what stops it producing something
harmful. Answer:

> The app sends a user-supplied photograph of a children's toy, plus the child's
> age in months, to the Anthropic Claude API and displays the structured result.
> The model is instructed not to give medical advice, never to claim a toy is
> certified safe, and to state in every response that its guidance does not
> replace safety certifications, packaging warnings or recall data. Output is
> constrained to a fixed JSON schema for the analysis. Chat answers are limited
> to the toy the user scanned. There is no free-form image generation, no
> user-to-user content, and nothing a user writes is shown to anyone else.
